Soft Magnetic Microrobot Doped with Porous Silica for Stability-Enhanced Multimodal Locomotion in a Nonideal Environment

Student Researcher · Lu Lab, Tsinghua University Chemical Engineering Lab of Industrial Catalysis · Jun 2022 – Oct 2022

Overview

Remotely controlled soft magnetic microrobots offer a promising approach to minimally invasive surgery by navigating the interior of the human body to perform targeted tasks with reduced trauma and complications. However, their clinical application has been limited by a fundamental challenge: most existing designs were developed and tested in idealized conditions, and struggle to maintain stable locomotion in the slippery, irregular human organ environment. To address this challenge, I worked with Shangsong Li and Professor Yuan Lu at Tsinghua Chemical Engineering Lab of Industrial Catalysis to engineer a porous silica-doped soft magnetic microrobot with enhanced locomotion stability across a range of non-ideal environments, expanding its potential for precision medicine applications including thrombus removal and biocatalysis. The work was published in ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces (2022).

Background & Significance

Soft magnetic microrobots can perform multimodal locomotion — rolling, walking, jumping, crawling, and drifting — under the control of an external magnetic field. This flexibility makes them attractive for biomedical applications such as minimally invasive surgery, targeted drug delivery, and cancer treatment. However, previous research largely demonstrated these locomotions in controlled, ideal environments: flat surfaces, still water, uniform substrates. In a real biological environment, the robot encounters muscle peristalsis, fluid flow, steep slopes, obstacles, and rapidly changing physical conditions — all of which can cause conventional microrobots to slip, stall, or fail entirely. Addressing this gap required not just a new locomotion design, but a fundamental material-level intervention to improve how the robot interacts with its environment.

Methods & Contributions

  • Tuned magnetic fields in Python and experimented with magnetization techniques to enable advanced locomotion modalities, such as crawling through confined tubular spaces for thrombus removal as a less traumatic alternative to conventional thrombectomy.
  • Tested 6 locomotion modes across multiple substrate materials (PTFE, silicone, PU) at varying p-Si concentrations to characterize how material composition influenced adhesion, stability, and motion performance in non-ideal environments.
  • Simulated in vivo topography — including human stomach and blood vessel environments — to plan and validate motion routes under realistic biological disturbance conditions.
  • Analyzed experimental data and captured and edited video documentation across all locomotion demonstrations, linking material composition to maneuverability and motion consistency for the published paper.

Research Skills

Material synthesis and characterization, tensile and adhesion mechanical testing, Python, soft robot fabrication, motion capture data analysis, scientific writing and video documentation.
