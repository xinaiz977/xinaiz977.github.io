Welcome to my portfolio!

Hi, I'm Xinai. I'm a Master's student in Mechanical Engineering at Stanford University, with a focus on mechatronics and product design.

I have always been passionate about hands-on building. I still remember how much I enjoyed my time playing Legos as a kid, and I specifically loved the moment of coming up with new ideas, mapping it out, and building my own creations. It wasn't just about assembling parts, but about exploring new ideas, testing them out, and refining the design. That is what makes engineering genuinely exciting to me.

So as I entered college, I decided to become an engineer. I have been very interested in healthcare technology, seeing how advanced technologies interface with the human body, and how they improve people's quality of life through different interactions. I also felt personally related to the field of biomedical engineering: since I love building things, I wanted to use my power in design and engineering to protect people that I love from disease and illnesses. So I decided to pursue a degree in biomedical engineering at Brown, and I was fortunate to have the opportunity to work on a variety of projects, from designing a wearable device for monitoring vital signs, to developing a robotic system for surgical assistance.

As I continued my undergraduate studies, I found myself most interested in the mechanical design and device development aspects among the broad field of biomedical engineering, and I wanted to further my knowledge and skills in this area, which is what drove me to Stanford. I'm currently a first year Master's in Mechanical Engineering, with a specific focus on mechatronics. I'm taking ME218 (Smart Product Design Fundamentals) and ME310 (Design Thinking) this quarter to explore two complementary aspects of product development. Through ME218, I'm excited to dive deeper into the technical side of mechatronics, including designing and integrating mechanical, electrical, and control systems. In the meantime, I look forward to learning the broader product design process from the industry perspective in ME310, from understanding user needs and ideation to prototyping, testing, and iterating on solutions to real-world problems.

I'm also actively exploring opportunities in product design and development across industries, where I can combine my background in mechanical design, biomechanical analysis, and instrumentation to help build products accessible and meaningful for users. If that resonates, I'd love to connect!
