Autonomous Light-Seeking Rover

By comparing light intensity detected by two oppositely-facing phototransistors, the rover determines its direction and adjusts its motors accordingly, navigating continuously through a custom-built state machine.

System Design

Mechanical Design

The rover consisted of DC motors mounted to a chassis and wired to an Adafruit Motor Shield (V2), which interfaced directly with the Arduino. This shield translated digital commands from the Arduino into motor operations, providing the physical means for the rover to move and turn.

Light-Sensing Circuit

To detect light direction, two PNZ-150 phototransistors were positioned facing opposite directions, each converting incoming light into an electrical current proportional to its intensity. Because the rover's two sides experienced different light levels depending on the source's position, the two phototransistors produced currents of differing magnitudes. A differential op-amp (AD8421) with a high common-mode rejection ratio compared these two currents and amplified their difference, producing a signal proportional to the direction and intensity of the light differential. The op-amp was powered through the Arduino's 5V and ground lines, connected to a 9V battery, with a virtual ground established for stable operation. A digital oscilloscope was used throughout to verify signal behavior and troubleshoot the circuit design.

Navigation Logic

The op-amp's output signal was fed into the Arduino, where it served as the basis for a custom state machine governing the rover's movement. I defined a set of motion states (e.g., moving forward, turning left, turning right) along with the threshold conditions that triggered transitions between them based on the magnitude and sign of the light differential signal. I wrote the control code in Arduino C++, linking the sensing circuit, state machine logic, and motor shield commands into a single cohesive control loop, allowing the rover to continuously sense its surroundings, evaluate its current state, and adjust its motors in real time — enabling it to autonomously track and follow a moving light source.

This project was developed by myself. I designed and built the light-sensing circuit, selecting and positioning the phototransistors and tuning the differential amplifier to reliably detect directional light differences. I also designed the rover's state machine, defining the motion states and transition logic based on sensor output, and wrote all of the Arduino control code that ties the sensing, decision-making, and motor control together into a functioning autonomous system. Verified signal integrity and debugged circuit issues with an oscilloscope, I refined the design through multiple iterations until the rover reliably tracked and followed a light source.

Skills: Analog circuit design, differential amplifier design, sensor integration, embedded systems programming (Arduino C++), state machine design, oscilloscope-based signal troubleshooting
